The book

Atomic Habits

James Clear's 2018 book on building good habits and breaking bad ones. Its core claim: you don't need bigger goals, you need a better system — habits are the compound interest of self-improvement, and getting 1% better each day, repeated for a year, adds up to something close to 37× better, not 365% better. Small and forgettable on any single day; enormous over time.

"You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems." A goal is a direction; a habit is the mechanism that actually gets you there — and the trackers in this corner of the site exist to make that mechanism visible, one day at a time.

The Four Laws of Behavior Change

Clear's framework for building a habit

  • 1. Make it obviousDesign the cue so it's impossible to miss.
  • 2. Make it attractivePair the habit with something you want.
  • 3. Make it easyReduce the friction between you and the action.
  • 4. Make it satisfyingGive yourself an immediate reward for doing it.

Break a bad habit by inverting all four: make the cue invisible, the habit unattractive, the action hard, and the outcome unsatisfying.

Identity, not outcomes

Clear's other big idea: habits aren't really about the outcome you're chasing, they're votes for the type of person you want to become. Skipping sugar once isn't really about that one afternoon — it's a small piece of evidence that you're someone who doesn't need it. Enough votes and the identity itself changes, which is what makes the habit stick once the streak-counter novelty wears off.
